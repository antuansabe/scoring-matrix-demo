# Ashoka Core Narratives

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Source of truth** for the `/about` page and the operational distillation in `lib/prompts/narrative-context.ts`. Keep in sync.

Ashoka's framework change strategy is structured around three key societal shifts, or **Core Narratives**. These narratives define the targets of theEveryone a Changemaker (EACH) paradigm shift.

---

## 1. Lifelong Contribution (formerly "Youth in Charge")
- **Conceptual Framing**: Agency and contribution are not delayed until adulthood. Contribution is a lifelong process that begins in youth.
- **Narrative Shift**: Moves from viewing young people as passive beneficiaries, objects of mentoring, or "future leaders" to acknowledging and structurally supporting them as active changemakers in the present tense.
- **Linguistic Traces**: Grammatical subject of active verbs is the young person; temporal framing is in the present tense; actions focus on enabling and distributing authority rather than mentoring or managing.

---

## 2. Changemaker Networks (formerly "Interconnected Teams")
- **Conceptual Framing**: Social changes are not the result of single, heroic founders. They occur through fluid, collaborative networks that span across traditional hierarchies.
- **Narrative Shift**: Moves from command-and-control hierarchical structures or isolated, delegative teams toward interconnected "equipos en red" (networks of teams) where roles are fluid, knowledge sharing is bidirectional, and leadership is shared across generations.
- **Linguistic Traces**: Vocabulary references collaboration, networks, sharing, role-switching; grammatical structures distribute agency across multiple actors; organizational structures are described as flat and porous.

---

## 3. Empathy-based Societies
- **Conceptual Framing**: Empathy is the cognitive foundation of changemaking. It is not a soft sentiment but an active, conscious competence that is used to diagnose and redesign social architectures.
- **Narrative Shift**: Moves from emotional sympathy ("feeling bad for others" or charity) to conscious, systemic empathy. Empathy in action involves using perspective-taking to identify root patterns of exclusion or barrier structures and dynamically adjusting the collective approach to address them.
- **Linguistic Traces**: Grammatical structures name barries or structures as subjects; reported speech shows others' perspectives having real epistemic weight; narratives show structural course corrections and perspective-driven design changes.
