# Scoring Model — Full Specification

Source of truth: `Stage1_Scoring_Model_FINAL` as developed by Giselle Kuri (Ashoka Framework Change). This document is a compact, code-ready summary. The narrative theory lives in the source doc.

## What it measures

The Changemaker Paradigm Scoring Matrix is a **paradigm enactment instrument**, not a sentiment tool. It measures the **discursive enactment** of changemaker identity — the degree to which the architecture of a text (its grammar of agency, framing of problems and solutions, construction of relationships, empathy and collaboration outlook, and enactment of identity) is structurally consistent with the changemaker paradigm.

It does NOT measure: how positive a text is, how inspiring it is, or "how much of a changemaker" the author is. We are not ranking changemakers; we are reading the linguistic traces the paradigm leaves — or does not leave.

## The five dimensions

Each dimension is scored 0–4. Each score has a named paradigm level.

### D1 — Agency & Contribution
*Drawn from: De Fina (narrative positioning), Van Dijk (ideological square), Entman (causal attribution). Ashoka: Everyone a Changemaker, Youth in Charge.*

**Question:** Does the text enact a world where every person — including and especially young people — is capable of contributing to the common good? Or does it reserve agency for adults, experts, and institutions?

**Youth bias built in:** a text with no youth-in-present-tense or intergenerational agency dimension can reach level 3, but cannot score above 3 on D1.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 0 | Agency belongs exclusively to the organization or narrator. Community members are objects of action. Contribution = receiving. |
| 1 | Community occasionally acts, but narrator holds epistemic authority. Youth as beneficiaries or future leaders. "We empower them." |
| 2 | Distributed agency across adult actors. Youth acknowledged but framed as future agents. Narrator still primary initiator. |
| 3 | Mutual contribution. "We" is genuinely collective and agency is distributed among any actors (adults included), without requiring youth or intergenerational presence. |
| 4 | Universal, structurally enacted agency. Level-3 profile with the addition of youth as present agents / youth in charge in the present tense. |

**Key linguistic signals:** grammatical subject of active verbs, temporal framing of youth (present vs. deferred), "common good" vocabulary, breadth of named actors, who is attributed problem-solving.

---

### D2 — Systemic & Architectural Framing
*Drawn from: Lakoff (deep frames), Entman (problem definition + causal attribution), Gee (big-D Discourse). Ashoka: COMPLETE Framework.*

**Question:** Does the text locate change at the level of rules, structures, and social architectures — or at the level of individuals and programs?

The 8 COMPLETE dimensions: **C**ultural, **O**rganizational, **M**etrics, **P**olicy/Governance, **L**egal, **E**conomic, **T**echnological, **E**nvironmental.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 0 | Problems are individual or behavioral. Solutions are services delivered to deficient people. Metaphors of rescue, charity, access. |
| 1 | Structural context acknowledged as backdrop, but solutions are still programmatic. Rules are named but not targeted. |
| 2 | At least one COMPLETE dimension is explicitly named or clearly implied as a site of change — not just a constraint. |
| 3 | Multiple architectural dimensions are targeted simultaneously. Change theory operates at field or system level. |
| 4 | Text demonstrates awareness that rules themselves need redesigning. "Architectures," "rules," "conditions for flourishing" are explicit targets. Interdependencies named. Second-order effects appear. Text holds complexity without premature resolution. |

**Key linguistic signals:** conceptual metaphors (journey/repair/cultivation/architecture redesign), deficit words vs. structural/barrier/rules words, scale of solution language, naming any COMPLETE dimension as a change target, complexity language.

---

### D3 — Empathy Enactment
*Drawn from: De Fina (level 2 & 3 positioning), Wodak (DHA: nomination + predication), Van Dijk (context models). Ashoka: Empatía Consciente.*

**Question:** Does the text demonstrate the practice of conscious empathy — the capacity to be aware of and understand our own and others’ perspectives, and to guide one’s actions to contribute to the common good — or does it stop at emotional solidarity?

**Critical threshold (between 2 and 3):** emotional empathy ("I care about you") vs. conscious empathy ("I used understanding of your perspective to identify a structural pattern and changed my approach").

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 0 | Others are categorized by deficit or need. No perspective-taking visible. Nomination strategies assign fixed, deficit-based identities. |
| 1 | Emotional solidarity present ("we care about") but perspectives not structurally integrated. Reported speech absent or paraphrased. |
| 2 | Others' perspectives represented with some fidelity, but narrator still interprets through their own frame. No evidence of changed approach. |
| 3 | Multiple perspectives with genuine epistemic weight. Narrator shows evidence of having changed direction, revised assumptions, identified patterns. Power dynamics begin to appear. |
| 4 | Conscious empathy structurally enacted. Text shows how understanding others reshaped the approach, identified systemic patterns, informed collective action. Power and privilege named and examined. |

**Key linguistic signals:** nomination strategies, predication, direct vs. indirect reported speech, evidence of perspective-driven course correction, power/privilege vocabulary, pattern recognition language.

---

### D4 — Collaboration & Leadership Model
*Drawn from: Fairclough (orders of discourse), De Fina (positioning), Tajfel (group identity). Ashoka: Colaboración Fluida, Liderazgo Abierto, equipos interconectados.*

**Question:** Does the text enact distributed, fluid leadership that shares power and knowledge across hierarchies — including across generations?

**Intergenerational test:** a text that models fluid collaboration only among adult peers (no substantive intergenerational dimension) can reach level 3, but cannot score above 3 on D4 (cannot reach level 4).

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 0 | Single leader / hero model. Collaboration is delegation. "I built / I created / I led." No intergenerational dimension. |
| 1 | Team acknowledged but narrator is clearly the driver. Collaboration = method narrator employs. Intergenerational = mentorship. |
| 2 | Multiple leaders named. Roles shared in description if not in grammar. Intergenerational mentioned but hierarchically structured. |
| 3 | Fluid role-switching. Knowledge-sharing bidirectional. Distributed leadership and fluid collaboration among any actors, without requiring youth or intergenerational presence. |
| 4 | Leadership structural and distributed across ages, roles, hierarchies. Level-3 profile with the addition of a substantive intergenerational dimension (youth and adults as co-contributors with power explicitly shared). |

**Key linguistic signals:** subject distribution across ages, role vocabulary (fixed vs. fluid), network language, knowledge-sharing markers and directionality, intergenerational markers, bidirectionality signals.

---

### D5 — Identity Embodiment
*Drawn from: De Fina (level 1 positioning, voicing devices, chronotopicity), Tajfel (social identity), narratives-as-practice, Gee (big-D Discourse as enacted identity). Ashoka: changemaker identity as practiced, not claimed.*

**Question:** Does the narrator position themselves as a changemaker through the **structure** of their language — or do they merely claim the label? Is the identity enacted consistently across time and context, or only when the frame is activated?

**Core principle:** A changemaker identity is enacted, not claimed. The metaphors, agency distribution, reflexivity, and handling of failure demonstrate the paradigm without requiring the label.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 0 | Narrator absent (institutional voice) OR present only as expert/hero/authority. No reflexivity. Identity fixed. |
| 1 | Personal voice present but instrumentalized (used to establish credibility). Changemaker vocabulary claimed without structural enactment. |
| 2 | Narrator positions self as part of collective process. Some reflexivity, but positionality and power not examined. |
| 3 | Narrator explicitly negotiates own identity in relation to the work. Positionality, power dynamics, learning named. Identity shown as evolving. Some polyphony. |
| 4 | Changemaker identity enacted, not claimed. Structure of language demonstrates paradigm without requiring the label. Identity is porous and evolving. Voicing is genuinely polyphonic. |

**Key linguistic signals:** claim vs. enactment gap, voicing devices (direct/reported speech, pronoun switches), reflexivity markers, temporal positioning (change over time), habituality signals, polyphony, identity negotiation.

## Paradigm names by score band

Applied to the overall Enactment Score (0–100):

| Score range | Paradigm name | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| 0–19 | **Spectator** | Change happens elsewhere, authored by others. |
| 20–39 | **Sympathizer** | Change is recognized and valued, but still understood as someone else's job. |
| 40–59 | **Contributor** | Agency enters the picture. The narrator sees themselves as a contributor, though the framing remains partial. |
| 60–79 | **Changemaker** | I am creating change. Change is owned, built, and driven from within. |
| 80–100 | **System Architect** | We are rewriting the rules. Agency is distributed, structures are named and challenged. |

The same naming applies at each dimension's individual 0–4 score for the radar.

## Weighting and formula

### Default weights
| Dimension | Weight | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| D1 — Agency & Contribution | **25%** | Strongest structural signal; hardest to fake; central to EACH and Lifelong Contribution. |
| D2 — Systemic & Architectural Framing | **25%** | Core to COMPLETE Framework; Ashoka's strategic differentiator. |
| D3 — Empathy Enactment | **20%** | First of the 4 skills; most detectable in language; key to Empathy-based Societies. |
| D4 — Collaboration & Leadership | **20%** | Second + third skills; intergenerational collaboration as explicit test. |
| D5 — Identity Embodiment | **10%** | Most context-dependent; most constrained by genre. |

### Genre-adjusted weights
| Genre | D1 | D2 | D3 | D4 | D5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Default | 25 | 25 | 20 | 20 | 10 |
| Free-form interview | 25 | 20 | 20 | 20 | 15 |
| Youth-focused text | 30 | 20 | 20 | 20 | 10 |
| Institutional report | 20 | 30 | 20 | 20 | 10 |
| Structured profile | 25 | 25 | 20 | 20 | 10 |
| Social media post | 30 | 20 | 30 | 10 | 10 |
| Speech / public address | 25 | 25 | 20 | 20 | 10 |
| Fundraising copy | 20 | 30 | 25 | 15 | 10 |

### Formula
```
Enactment Score (0–100) = (D1·w1 + D2·w2 + D3·w3 + D4·w4 + D5·w5) × 25
```
where each Dn is 0–4 and weights sum to 1.0.

### Minimum word counts (PARTIAL SCORE flag below threshold)
- D1, D2, D3: 150 words
- D4: 300 words
- D5: 400 words

## EACH Orientation

After scoring, the dimensional profile is read against Ashoka's three Key Societal Shifts to determine the EACH (Everyone a Changemaker) Orientation.

| If text scores highest on… | Primary orientation toward… |
|---|---|
| D1 + D5 (both ≥ 3, and these are the top two) | **Lifelong Contribution** |
| D2 + D4 (both ≥ 3, top two) | **Changemaker Networks** |
| D3 + D1 (both ≥ 3, top two) | **Empathy-based Societies** |
| All five ≥ 3 with no two clearly dominant | **Full EACH Alignment** |
| None of above (overall score < 60 or no clear dominance) | **Emerging** |

**Note on overlap:** A text high on D1 + D3 + D5 may trigger both Lifelong Contribution and Empathy-based Societies. Surface this honestly — show the dual orientation rather than forcing a single label. This is a known open question in the model (Q-overlap).

## Validation protocol (informational, not implemented in demo)

The model uses **face validity against an expert pre-scored anchor corpus** rather than human inter-rater reliability. Target: AI-to-expert agreement ≥ 0.75 on every dimension across a 10–15 text anchor corpus.

For the v0.1 demo this means: the 4 sample texts in `docs/SAMPLES.md` carry **expert pre-scores from Giselle**. The live scorer (Claude via the API route) is being shown to the expert; she validates whether its readings match her readings.

## Out of scope for v0.1

- **CMDR (Changemaker Density Rate)**: requires aggregating multiple individuals. Not in this demo.
- **Cross-Genre Coherence Flag**: requires multiple texts from same author. Not in this demo.
- **Confidence calibration**: surfaced as a flag, but not formally calibrated.
